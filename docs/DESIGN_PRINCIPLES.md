# Design principles

Dear Alex uses self-distancing: users give compassionate advice to a fictional character before seeing that the advice applies to them. It is a small self-help reflection tool, not therapy or a medical device.

## Safety and privacy

Free text is checked before it is stored. Clear crisis language ends the game and presents Canadian 9-8-8 and UVic Counselling support; flagged text is never persisted. There are no accounts, analytics, or runtime external API calls. The user can export or delete all device-scoped data.

## Deliberate omissions

No streaks, XP decay, leaderboards, notifications, popups, countdowns, paywalls, or shame mechanics. Every step includes a penalty-free “Not today” exit. Reduced motion is respected and audio never plays automatically.

## Research basis and limits

The mechanism draws on self-distancing research, including Kross et al. (2014), Grossmann and Kross (2014), and Kross and Ayduk (2017). This is not clinical treatment and cannot assess risk beyond routing explicit crisis phrasing to immediate support.
