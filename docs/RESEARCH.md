# Research Basis

Dear Alex is grounded in peer-reviewed self-distancing research. This document summarises the three primary studies, explains what each one shows, and maps each finding to a specific design decision in the app.

---

## What is self-distancing?

Self-distancing is the practice of stepping outside your own perspective when thinking about a difficult situation — viewing the event as an observer rather than as yourself. Research shows this shift can reduce emotional reactivity, promote wiser reasoning, and support more adaptive self-talk, without requiring professional intervention.

---

## Primary studies

### [1] Kross, E., Bruehlman-Senecal, E., Park, J., Burson, A., Dougherty, A., Sankaran, S., … Ayduk, O. (2014). Self-talk as a regulatory mechanism: How you do it matters. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 106*(2), 304–324.

**What it shows.** When people use their own name (rather than "I") during self-reflection, they achieve psychological distance equivalent to reflecting on a *friend's* problem. Third-person self-talk reduces emotional reactivity in real time, without requiring effortful reappraisal strategies.

**Design connection.** Dear Alex formalises this by asking users to write *about Alex* rather than about themselves. The story engine converts first-person text to third person, creating the linguistic distance Kross et al. measured. The Reveal then inverts this: the same text re-emerges in the user's own words, so they keep the wisdom they generated from the distanced perspective.

---

### [2] Grossmann, I., & Kross, E. (2014). Exploring Solomon's paradox: Self-distancing eliminates the self-other asymmetry in wise reasoning about close relationships in younger and older adults. *Psychological Science, 25*(8), 1571–1580.

**What it shows.** People reason more wisely about *others'* problems than their own — the so-called Solomon's Paradox. Grossmann and Kross showed that self-distancing (analysing your own problem as if it were someone else's) eliminates this asymmetry. Distanced reasoners showed more intellectual humility, considered multiple perspectives, and anticipated change better.

**Design connection.** The three coaching scenes (The Moment, The Voice, Tomorrow) are structured around the exact reasoning components Grossmann and Kross measured: situational acknowledgement, inner-critic reframing, and forward action. Asking "What does *Alex* need?" instead of "What do *I* need?" activates the same distancing mechanism their study isolates.

---

### [3] Kross, E., & Ayduk, O. (2017). Self-distancing: Theory, research, and current directions. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 55*, 81–136.

**What it shows.** This review synthesises a decade of self-distancing research. Key findings: (a) distancing works across cultures and age groups; (b) it is effective for *negative* affect specifically, not neutral events; (c) the benefits persist without professional guidance; (d) distancing does not suppress emotion — it reprocesses it adaptively.

**Design connection.** (a) Dear Alex uses a universal fictional character (Alex) with no cultural or demographic assumptions. (b) The app is targeted at setback moments, not positive or neutral events — consistent with when the mechanism is most effective. (c) No clinician or coach is required; the user's own language is the resource. (d) No advice card in the deck asks users to dismiss or suppress feelings; every Compassion card validates the difficulty first.

## Gamification and the research

Self-distancing is a *mechanism*, not a game. Dear Alex wraps it in four lightweight mechanics — narrative, creativity, collection, and surprise — chosen to support repeated engagement without pressure:

| Mechanic | Research link |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** (coaching Alex through 3 scenes) | Creates the linguistic/perspectival distance Kross et al. measured |
| **Creativity** (free-text advice, custom character name) | Self-generated advice has stronger personal relevance than received advice |
| **Collection** (Compassion / Perspective / Action slots, never lost) | Reward without loss aversion; no shame if a day is skipped |
| **Surprise** (scored resurfacing of a past letter) | Delivers the user's own past wisdom at a moment when it is useful |

Deliberate omissions — streaks, XP decay, leaderboards, daily push notifications — were excluded because they create conditions (guilt on missed days, external comparison) that conflict with the goal of reducing harsh self-judgment.

---

## Limitations

- Dear Alex is a self-help tool, not clinical treatment.
- The safety gate routes explicit crisis language to professional support (9-8-8, UVic Counselling) but cannot assess risk beyond that.
- The story engine uses rule-based first-to-third-person conversion. It covers common English constructions; unusual phrasing falls back to a quoted template.
- The app has not been clinically validated or subject to an ethics review. Future work could include user research with appropriate ethics oversight.
